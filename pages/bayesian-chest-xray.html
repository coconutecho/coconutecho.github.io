Partial Bayesian Networks for Chest X-Ray Classification

Role: Co-author & corresponding author
With: Yuanchen Wang (Harvard Medical School; USC)
Tools: PyTorch, torchbnn, scikit-optimize, Captum, OpenCV
Duration: Jun 2025 – Apr 2026

Manuscript · Wang, Y. & Xu, W. Partial Bayesian Approach for Chest X-Ray Image Classification: Balancing Accuracy and Uncertainty.

Why

In medical imaging, a model that says how sure it is can matter as much as one that is right. Bayesian neural networks treat weights as distributions rather than fixed values, so they return a spread of predictions, flag low-confidence cases, and regularize well when data are limited.

Making a whole deep network Bayesian is expensive, though: memory grows, training becomes unstable, and fully Bayesian models often fail to beat ordinary ones. Few studies have compared, under controlled conditions, how much of a network is worth making Bayesian.

What

We trained three ResNet-50 variants that differ only in how much of the network is Bayesian, and compared them on pneumonia detection from chest X-rays.

  • Non-Bayesian: every layer deterministic (blocks A and B in the diagram above).
  • Partial-Bayesian: only the final linear classification layer (block B) is Bayesian.
  • Fully Bayesian: every convolutional and linear layer is probabilistic; batch normalization and pooling stay deterministic.

How

Data

A public pediatric chest X-ray dataset (Kermany, 2018) with two classes, normal and pneumonia: 5,200 training images (1,333 normal, 3,867 pneumonia), 32 validation images, and 624 test images. Pneumonia outnumbers normal almost three to one in training, so weighted random sampling kept every mini-batch roughly balanced.

Preprocessing

Standard ResNet-50 preprocessing (resize to 232, center-crop to 224, ImageNet normalization), with Gaussian blur, random perspective, and random affine augmentation on the training set only. Each model was trained twice, on the original images and on images enhanced with contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE).

Models

All variants start from ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 weights. Bayesian layers (torchbnn) model each weight as a Gaussian with prior mean 0 and standard deviation 0.1, a prior chosen by inspecting the pretrained weight distributions. Pretrained weights initialize the posterior means. At evaluation, Bayesian models run many stochastic forward passes and average them.

Training and tuning

Loss = (1 − p) · NLL + p · KL. The KL weight p, batch size, optimizer, and learning rate were chosen per model by Gaussian-process Bayesian optimization (scikit-optimize): 25 iterations, each scored by mean validation F1 across three random 90/10 splits. Final models trained for 50 epochs.

Explainability

Integrated Gradients (Captum), from a black-image baseline, show which image regions drive each prediction: is the model looking at the lungs or at background cues?

  • A Bayesian output layer was enough. The partial-Bayesian model scored highest on both accuracy and F1, ahead of the deterministic baseline, and trained only about 15% slower.
  • Full Bayesianization hurt. With 23M+ parameters turned into distributions and the same 50-epoch budget, the fully Bayesian model stayed near 70% accuracy. Its Monte Carlo predictions were still consistent, and narrower when correct, which points to slow convergence rather than a useless model.
  • Uncertainty came for free. On a normal X-ray, 1,000 forward passes of the partial-Bayesian model gave a mean normal-class probability of 93.6% (SD 4.2) with a 90% interval of [85.8%, 95.3%]: a prediction with its own error bars.
  • CLAHE helped the deterministic model, not the Bayesian one. Contrast enhancement lifted the non-Bayesian model but slightly lowered partial-Bayesian accuracy. It did, however, shrink the partial model’s prediction variance, a trade-off between raw accuracy and stable predictions.
  • The strong models looked at the lungs. Integrated Gradients maps for the best configurations concentrated on consolidated lung regions; the fully Bayesian model failed to localize them.

Limitations and next steps

  • One task and one dataset. Whether partial-Bayesian gains carry over to other modalities and diseases is still open.
  • A fixed epoch budget may have under-trained the fully Bayesian model; longer or adaptive schedules are the obvious next test.
  • Uncertainty was judged from predictive distributions and intervals. Formal calibration metrics, such as expected calibration error and coverage, would make the reliability case stronger.
  • Bayesian layers placed earlier or mid-network, and cheaper variational approximations, are natural extensions.
