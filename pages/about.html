About

I study how people think and behave, and I build the machine learning models that help measure it.

I’m a Ph.D. student in Psychology at the University of Southern California, specializing in quantitative methods, computational psychology, and developmental psychology. Alongside the Ph.D., I’m completing a part-time M.S. in Computer Science at Georgia Tech, focused on machine learning.

My work spans representation learning for large-scale user data, uncertainty-aware deep learning for medical imaging, and LLM-assisted NLP for open-ended survey responses. Across all of it, I care about careful experimental design, honest evaluation, and models whose outputs people can trust and interpret.

Most recently I was a Machine Learning Engineer Intern on Epsilon’s Decision Science team, where I built user embeddings serving 100M+ users. Before graduate school I studied Cognitive Science at the University of Pennsylvania, concentrating in Computation and Cognition.
